# Distribution playbook

How findings and tools reach people. Not how the homepage reaches people.

The single most common mistake is promoting the site. Nobody wants a website.
They want to know why their bins cost more than the next council's, and the
site is where that answer happens to live.

## The model

**One national release, thirty-two local ones.**

Every dataset that covers all 32 councils is 33 stories: one Scotland-wide, and
one for each council that belongs to a specific newsroom's patch. The national
one gets covered once. The local ones each get covered in a paper that has no
other source for them.

Local outlets are the higher-yield half and almost nobody does the work, which
is why it works.

## What to promote

| Promote | Do not promote |
| --- | --- |
| A finding with a number in it | The site |
| A calculator that answers one question | The homepage |
| A local page for a named council | The council index |
| A dataset a journalist can open | A press release about a dataset |

## Local journalist outreach

The Local Democracy Reporting Service funds reporters embedded in local
newsrooms whose whole job is covering councils. They file constantly and they
are the right first contact. Their work is bylined "Local Democracy Reporter",
so recent council coverage in a target paper names the person to write to.

### The pitch

Under 150 words. The finding in the subject line. No attachment, because a
first email with one goes to spam.

```
Subject: [Council] has the [worst thing] in Scotland: figures and source

Hi [name],

[Council] [the finding, with the number and the Scotland comparison].

It comes from [the source], the national comparison every council takes part
in, so every council is measured the same way.

Full breakdown, every figure linked to the document it came from:
scotlandcounted.org.uk/councils/[slug]

Free to use, credit Scotland Counted. I can send the spreadsheet covering all
32 councils if that helps.

[name]
Scotland Counted
```

### Rules

- **Search the outlet before sending.** If they have already run it, say so in
  the first line and pitch the follow-up. A pitch that ignores their own
  coverage gets deleted.
- **Five a week, not thirty-two at once.** You learn what lands, you can answer
  replies properly, and once one runs you can open the next email with that.
- **One nudge after three to five working days, then stop.** Chasing twice
  annoys the person who might run your next story.
- **Right of reply is the journalist's job, not yours.** Approaching the
  council yourself tips them off before publication. Say instead that officers
  can respond on the page and corrections are logged, which is a fact about the
  site rather than an offer to act.
- **Never claim an exclusive on something already published.** Check the date of
  the underlying release first.

## Local Facebook groups

High volume, and the fastest way to get a domain blocked if done wrong.

- Post the fact, not the link. The link is the source, not the pitch.
- Better, join the argument that already exists. Every one of these groups has a
  running thread about bins, potholes or council tax.
- Best, have somebody local post it. A community councillor sharing it is news.
  You sharing it is promotion.
- Time it to the day the local paper runs the story.
- Read each group's rules first, and never post the same wording twice.

One ban is survivable. A pattern of them gets the domain flagged by Facebook
itself, which is very hard to undo.

## WhatsApp

The most-used sharing channel in Britain and the least measurable. Optimise for
it by making the first line of a page repeatable out loud. A page whose opening
sentence contains a number and a comparison gets forwarded. A page that opens
with context does not.

## Reddit

r/Scotland is large and politically engaged. Post as a person who found
something, say plainly that you built it, and answer every comment. The data
will be stress-tested in public, and surviving that is the point.

## Bluesky, X and LinkedIn

- **X** rewards an audience you already have. With no followers it is a
  retention channel, not an acquisition one. Post, but spend a tenth of the
  effort, and treat it as somewhere for people to land after coverage.
- **Bluesky** has a smaller but disproportionately journalist-heavy Scottish
  political community. Worth more per post than X at this stage.
- **LinkedIn** reaches council officers, civil servants and third-sector
  researchers. A methods post does better there than a finding.

## YouTube and short video

One explainer per dataset, two minutes, one finding. The description carries the
source link and the citation. Short video works for a single comparison with two
numbers and does not work for anything needing a caveat, which rules out most of
what this site publishes.

## Partnerships

Campaign and advice organisations cite council and poverty figures constantly
and mostly do it from memory. They are slow to convert and they link, which is
what fixes search visibility.

The approach is not "please share this". It is: *you cite council figures in
your briefings, here they are free and sourced, what would you need me to add
for you to use them?* That question gets answered when a request for a share
does not.

## Publishing rhythm

| When | What |
| --- | --- |
| Each data release | One national pitch, then five local pitches a week until the set is done |
| Weekly | One explainer or investigation |
| Monthly | One dataset refresh, logged on /updates |
| Quarterly | One flagship analysis |

## What should never be automated

- Sending pitch emails
- Posting to social accounts
- Any claim about a named politician or official
- Anything generated from data without a person reading it first

A generator may assemble figures into a template. It may not decide what the
figures mean.

## Review requirements

Anything naming a person or a party gets read twice before it leaves, once for
accuracy and once for tone. Anything alleging failure needs the source document
open at the time of writing, not remembered.

## Paid promotion

Not until something has worked organically. Paying to distribute a finding
nobody shared for free buys reach without learning anything.

## Templates

### Journalist email
See the pitch above.

### Local social post

```
[Council] spends [figure] on [thing]. The Scottish average is [figure].

That is [rank] of all 32 councils.

Figures from [source], linked here: [url]
```

### National social post

```
[N] of Scotland's 32 councils [finding].

[The counterpoint that makes it interesting.]

Every figure linked to its source: [url]
```

### YouTube description

```
[One-sentence finding.]

Source: [publisher], [document title], [date]
Full working, all 32 councils: [url]
Data: scotlandcounted.org.uk/data

Free to reuse with credit. Corrections: scotlandcounted.org.uk/corrections
```

### Source and correction note

```
Figures from [publisher], [document], [date]. Every number on the page links
to the document it came from. If something here is wrong, report it at
scotlandcounted.org.uk/corrections and it will be fixed and logged publicly.
```
